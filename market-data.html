What fertility treatment actually costs

Published IVF, IUI, egg-freezing, and donor-treatment price ranges from national cost guides and a university clinic cash-pay schedule, plus state insurance coverage counts.

Fertility pricing is hard to compare because clinics bundle different items. One clinic's quoted cycle fee may cover monitoring, retrieval, fertilization, and transfer, while medications, genetic testing, anesthesia, and a later frozen transfer are billed separately. The rows below therefore state what each figure includes wherever the source states it, and keep national ranges separate from one clinic's published schedule. A national range is not a quote, and a single clinic's schedule is not a national average. Most people who proceed need more than one cycle, which is why the total cost to reach a live birth runs well above any single-cycle figure.

How to read these numbers

Included: national price ranges from a 2025 cost guide attributing its ranges to FertilityIQ and GoodRx, a single university clinic's published cash-pay schedule with its discount and inclusions stated, a multi-cycle total-cost estimate, and RESOLVE state mandate counts. Excluded: individual clinic quotes, financing terms, and success-rate claims — prices say nothing about outcomes, and outcomes vary sharply by age, diagnosis, and protocol. National ranges and single-clinic schedules are kept in separate rows and never averaged: the university schedule sits above the national range for IVF because it bundles PGT and anesthesia and reflects one high-cost market. State mandate counts describe laws, not benefits on any one plan; self-funded employer plans are generally exempt from state mandates, and mandate-to-offer laws differ from mandate-to-cover laws.
